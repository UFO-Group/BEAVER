### Comprehensive Overview  
Crystallinity fundamentally governs the degradation behavior of biodegradable polymers by modulating water diffusion, chain scission kinetics, and erosion pathways. Processing techniques (thermal, mechanical, or solvent-based) directly manipulate crystallinity, enabling tailored degradation profiles. For implants, this interplay dictates mechanical integrity, degradation product release kinetics, and functional lifetime, with critical implications for clinical performance.  

---

### In-depth Molecular & Scientific Mechanisms  
**1. Why Crystallinity Affects Degradation**  
Biodegradable polymers (e.g., polyesters like PLA, PCL, PBS) exhibit semicrystalline structures with alternating amorphous and crystalline regions. Degradation occurs via hydrolysis, where water penetrates the polymer matrix to cleave hydrolytically labile bonds (e.g., ester groups). The rate-limiting steps are:  
- **Water Diffusion**: Amorphous regions have loosely packed chains with high free volume, enabling rapid water ingress. Crystalline regions feature tightly packed, ordered chains that sterically hinder water diffusion [1, 2, 3, 4, 5].  
- **Chain Scission**: Hydrolysis preferentially targets amorphous zones due to greater chain mobility and accessibility [1, 2, 3, 6]. Crystalline domains resist scission as chain alignment and hydrogen bonding stabilize the structure [1, 5].  
- **Erosion Dynamics**: Degradation initiates in amorphous regions, creating pores that expose crystalline domains to erosion. Larger/more stable crystals further retard degradation [1, 7].  

*Quantitative Evidence*:  
- PLA with 30% crystallinity degraded 2× slower than amorphous PLA (60% vs. 30% mass loss in 35 vs. 60 days) [8].  
- Poly(butylene adipate) (PBA) α-crystals degrade faster than β-crystals despite larger size, highlighting polymorphism’s role [7].  

**2. How Processing Modifies Crystallinity**  
Processing alters nucleation, crystal growth, and morphology, directly impacting crystallinity:  
- **Thermal Annealing**: Heating near melting temperature ($T_m$) followed by slow cooling promotes crystal perfection and increases crystallinity. *Example*: PLA annealed at 105°C for 8 h reached 30% crystallinity [8].  
- **Mechanical Stretching**: Uniaxial/biaxial drawing aligns chains, inducing orientation-dependent crystallization. Stretching increases crystallinity and lamellar thickness while reducing spherulite size [9, 10, 11].  
- **Blending/Additives**: Immiscible blends (e.g., PBS/PLA) alter crystallization kinetics via interfacial interactions. Nucleating agents (e.g., in PLLA) accelerate crystallization rates and increase crystallinity [11, 12, 13].  
- **Solvent Casting**: Evaporation rate controls crystal nucleation; slower rates favor larger crystals [14, 15].  

*Key Mechanisms*:  
- Chain mobility during processing determines crystal nucleation density.  
- Shear forces in extrusion/injection molding promote heterogeneous nucleation [11, 13].  

---

### Evidence Synthesis & Literature Trends  
1. **Crystallinity-Degradation Correlation**:  
   - 10/13 sources confirm an inverse relationship between crystallinity and degradation rate [1–7,9–11].  
   - Enzymatic degradation of PBSA showed a 40% reduction in rate when crystallinity increased by 15% [16].  
   - Degradation kinetics follow a two-stage process: initial amorphous erosion increases crystallinity (e.g., PGA [17]), followed by crystalline breakdown.  

2. **Processing-Degradation Links**:  
   - Stretched PLLA exhibits 25% slower hydrolysis than unstretched samples due to oriented crystals [9].  
   - Blending PLA with PBS under shear shifts crystallization phases, altering degradation profiles [12].  

3. **Implant-Specific Findings**:  
   - **Lifetime Trade-offs**: High crystallinity extends mechanical integrity but risks late-stage fragmentation ("flaking") when crystalline regions erode [17, 18].  
   - **Acidic Burst Hazard**: Fast-degrading polymers (e.g., PGA) release acidic monomers abruptly, causing inflammation [19, 20].  
   - **Degradation-Environment Interplay**: *In vivo* mechanical loading accelerates erosion in semicrystalline implants [18].  

---

### Limitations & Uncertainties  
1. **Nonlinear Dynamics**: Crystallinity may increase initially during degradation (e.g., PGA [17]), complicating lifetime predictions.  
2. **Polymorphism Effects**: Crystal structure (α vs. β) can override crystallinity trends (e.g., PBA [7]), but predictive models are lacking.  
3. **Processing-Property Gaps**: Optimal crystallinity for implants varies by polymer (e.g., PCL requires <50% for vascular grafts [20]).  
4. *In Vivo* Complexity**: Enzymatic activity, pH shifts, and mechanical stress modulate degradation unpredictably [18, 21].
