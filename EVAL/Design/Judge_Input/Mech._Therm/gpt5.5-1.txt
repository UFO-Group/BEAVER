A good strategy is a **segmented PLA-based copolymer** that separates the functions:

- **Rigid PLA-rich hard segments** → raise/retain high \(T_g\), stiffness, heat resistance.
- **Low-\(T_g\) biodegradable soft segments** → give ductility and tear resistance without making the whole material brittle.

## Proposed copolymer

### Structure

\[
\mathrm{P[(LA\text{-}co\text{-}IB)_m\text{-}b\text{-}(CL\text{-}co\text{-}TMC)_n]_x}
\]

where:

- **LA** = L-lactide, PLA-forming unit  
- **IB** = isosorbide-derived lactide/carbonate unit  
- **CL** = ε-caprolactone  
- **TMC** = trimethylene carbonate  

A practical target composition:

| Component | Role | Suggested content |
|---|---:|---:|
| L-lactide | Main biodegradable PLA backbone | 70–80 wt% |
| Isosorbide-derived cyclic carbonate/lactide | Rigid biobased unit to raise \(T_g\) | 5–10 mol% of hard segment |
| PCL/PTMC soft block | Ductility, impact strength | 10–20 wt% |
| Optional D-lactide | Control crystallinity/transparency | 1–3 mol% |

A representative formulation:

\[
\mathrm{P[(L\text{-}LA_{0.92}\text{-}co\text{-}IB_{0.08})\text{-}b\text{-}(CL_{0.7}\text{-}co\text{-}TMC_{0.3})]}
\]

with roughly **85 wt% PLA-rich hard phase** and **15 wt% soft phase**.

---

## Why this design works

### 1. Raising \(T_g\)

Pure PLA has a typical glass transition:

\[
T_g \approx 55{-}60^\circ \mathrm{C}
\]

Introducing a rigid bicyclic monomer such as **isosorbide** restricts chain rotation and increases free-volume constraints. This can raise the hard-phase \(T_g\) to approximately:

\[
T_g \approx 65{-}75^\circ \mathrm{C}
\]

depending on isosorbide content and molecular weight.

Isosorbide is attractive because it is:

- biobased,
- rigid,
- relatively transparent,
- compatible with polyester/carbonate chemistry.

### 2. Maintaining ductility

If you simply increase PLA rigidity, the material becomes more brittle. Therefore, the copolymer should not be a fully random rigid PLA copolymer. Instead, use a **block or multiblock architecture**.

The soft **PCL/PTMC segments** have very low \(T_g\), typically below room temperature, so they act as internal toughening domains. They allow plastic deformation, crazing/shear yielding, and energy dissipation during film stretching or impact.

The goal is to create a morphology like:

\[
\text{rigid PLA-rich matrix} + \text{finely dispersed biodegradable soft domains}
\]

rather than a single overly softened phase.

## Target properties for packaging film

| Property | Neat PLA | Target copolymer |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(T_g\) | 55–60°C | 65–75°C hard-phase \(T_g\) |
| Tensile modulus | 2–3 GPa | 1.5–2.5 GPa |
| Elongation at break | 3–10% | 100–250% |
| Tensile strength | 50–70 MPa | 35–60 MPa |
| Transparency | Good | Good if domains are nanoscale |
| Compostability | Good | Good if soft block content is controlled |

## Suggested synthesis route

### Step 1: Prepare soft macroinitiator

Use hydroxyl-terminated PCL/PTMC diol:

\[
\mathrm{HO\text{-}P(CL\text{-}co\text{-}TMC)\text{-}OH}
\]

with \(M_n \approx 2{,}000{-}5{,}000\ \mathrm{g/mol}\).

### Step 2: Ring-opening polymerization

Polymerize L-lactide with a small amount of isosorbide-derived cyclic carbonate or isosorbide-lactide monomer from the soft macroinitiator:

\[
\mathrm{LA + IB + HO\text{-}soft\text{-}OH \rightarrow PLA/IB\text{-}b\text{-}soft\text{-}b\text{-}PLA/IB}
\]

Use catalysts such as:

- tin(II) octoate, common industrially,
- or organocatalysts such as TBD/DBU for lower metal residue.

### Step 3: Optional chain extension

To increase melt strength for blown or cast film extrusion, use a food-contact-suitable multifunctional chain extender, for example:

- epoxide-functional chain extender,
- carbodiimide chain extender,
- or citric-acid-derived branching agent.

This improves film processability and tear strength.

---

## Recommended formulation

A strong starting candidate would be:

\[
\boxed{
\text{PLA-rich hard segment: } 92\ \mathrm{mol\%}\ L\text{-LA} + 8\ \mathrm{mol\%}\ isosorbide\ unit
}
\]

combined with:

\[
\boxed{
15\ \mathrm{wt\%}\ P(CL\text{-}co\text{-}TMC)\ soft\ segment
}
\]

Overall:

\[
\boxed{
85\ \mathrm{wt\%}\ P(LA\text{-}co\text{-}IB) + 15\ \mathrm{wt\%}\ P(CL\text{-}co\text{-}TMC)
}
\]

This should give a PLA-based biodegradable packaging resin with:

- higher effective heat resistance than neat PLA,
- improved toughness and ductility,
- good transparency if morphology is well controlled,
- retained biodegradability/compostability.
