### Proposed material: high-`Tg`, ductile PLA-based segmented copolymer

Design a **PLA-based ABA triblock or multiblock copolymer**:

\[
\text{P(LLA-co-isosorbide carbonate)}\;-\;b\; \text{P(TMC-co-CL)}\;-\;b\;\text{P(LLA-co-isosorbide carbonate)}
\]

where:

- **LLA** = L-lactide, the main PLA-forming monomer  
- **Isosorbide carbonate/lactide-type comonomer** = rigid bio-based cyclic comonomer to raise `Tg`  
- **TMC** = trimethylene carbonate  
- **CL** = ε-caprolactone  
- Architecture = hard–soft–hard segmented copolymer

A practical composition would be:

| Segment | Suggested content | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| PLA-rich hard blocks | 65–80 wt% | Strength, stiffness, biodegradability |
| Isosorbide-derived rigid units | 5–15 mol% of hard block | Increase `Tg` and heat resistance |
| P(TMC-co-CL) soft midblock | 20–35 wt% | Maintain ductility and tear resistance |

---

## Why this design works

### 1. Raising `Tg`

Neat PLA typically has:

\[
T_g \approx 55–60^\circ C
\]

Incorporating a rigid bicyclic bio-based monomer such as **isosorbide** restricts chain mobility. This can raise the hard-phase `Tg` into the approximate range:

\[
T_g \approx 65–80^\circ C
\]

depending on isosorbide content and molecular weight.

The isosorbide unit is attractive because it is:

- Bio-derived, usually from glucose/sorbitol
- Rigid and bulky
- Compatible with biodegradable polyester/carbonate chemistry
- Effective at increasing thermal resistance

---

### 2. Maintaining ductility

Adding only rigid comonomers to PLA would likely make the material more brittle. To avoid that, include a soft biodegradable midblock such as:

\[
\text{P(TMC-co-CL)}
\]

This segment has a low `Tg`, often below room temperature, so it acts as an internal toughening phase. It improves:

- Elongation at break
- Tear resistance
- Impact toughness
- Film flexibility

The hard PLA-rich blocks provide physical crosslinking domains, while the soft midblock dissipates stress.

---

## Target properties for biodegradable packaging film

Approximate design targets:

| Property | Target |
|---|---:|
| Hard-phase `Tg` | 65–80°C |
| Tensile strength | 35–60 MPa |
| Elongation at break | >100%, ideally 200–400% |
| Young’s modulus | 1–2.5 GPa |
| Optical clarity | High if crystallinity is controlled |
| Compostability | Better than petroleum-based packaging, depending on formulation |

---

## Recommended composition

A good starting formulation:

\[
\textbf{75 wt% P(LLA-co-10 mol% isosorbide carbonate)}\;-\;b\;\textbf{25 wt% P(TMC-co-CL)}
\]

with the soft segment ratio:

\[
\text{TMC:CL} = 70:30
\]

This keeps the soft phase flexible while avoiding excessive crystallinity from PCL.

---

## Synthesis route

Use controlled ring-opening polymerization:

1. Polymerize TMC and CL to form a hydroxyl-terminated soft macroinitiator:

\[
\text{HO-P(TMC-co-CL)-OH}
\]

2. Add L-lactide and an isosorbide-derived cyclic carbonate or lactide-like comonomer.

3. Grow PLA-rich hard blocks from both ends to form an ABA triblock:

\[
\text{P(LLA-co-isosorbide carbonate)}-b-\text{P(TMC-co-CL)}-b-\text{P(LLA-co-isosorbide carbonate)}
\]

4. Use a tin-free or low-toxicity catalyst for packaging applications, such as:

- Zinc lactate
- Magnesium alkoxides
- Organocatalysts such as TBD or DBU/thiourea systems

---

## Additional design notes

To improve packaging performance:

- Use mostly **L-lactide**, but include a small amount of **D-lactide or meso-lactide**, around 2–5 mol%, to reduce excessive crystallinity and improve transparency.
- Keep isosorbide content moderate. Too much will raise `Tg` but reduce processability and elongation.
- Target high molecular weight, preferably:

\[
M_n > 80{,}000 \text{ g/mol}
\]

for good film toughness.

---

## Summary design

A strong candidate is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{P(LLA-co-isosorbide carbonate)}-b-\text{P(TMC-co-CL)}-b-\text{P(LLA-co-isosorbide carbonate)}
}
\]

with approximately:

- 70–80 wt% PLA-rich hard phase  
- 5–15 mol% isosorbide-derived rigid comonomer in the hard phase  
- 20–30 wt% biodegradable soft carbonate/ester phase  

This design increases the effective `Tg` and heat resistance relative to PLA while preserving ductility needed for biodegradable packaging films.
