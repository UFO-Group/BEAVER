To design a PLA-based copolymer that simultaneously increases the glass transition temperature ($T_g$) and maintains ductility for biodegradable packaging, one must overcome the inherent stiffness-toughness trade-off. Increasing $T_g$ typically requires restricting chain mobility (making the material more brittle), while increasing ductility typically requires increasing chain flexibility (lowering $T_g$).

The optimal design strategy is a **Segmented Multiblock Copolymer** architecture that utilizes **microphase separation**. This allows the material to possess distinct "hard" and "soft" phases, where the hard phase dictates the thermal properties ($T_g$) and the soft phase dictates the mechanical toughness (ductility).

### Design Specification

**1. Architecture:**
**Segmented Multiblock Copolymer** (e.g., $(Hard)_n-(Soft)_n$).
*   **Reasoning:** A random copolymer would average the properties, likely lowering the $T_g$. A block copolymer allows for phase separation, preserving the high $T_g$ of the hard segments while the soft segments absorb impact energy.

**2. Hard Segment (High $T_g$ Phase):**
**Poly(L-lactide-co-isosorbide) (PLI).**
*   **Composition:** L-Lactide copolymerized with a small molar percentage (e.g., 5–15%) of **Isosorbide** (1,4:3,6-dianhydrohexitol).
*   **Function:** Isosorbide is a rigid, bicyclic, bio-based diol. Its incorporation into the PLA backbone significantly restricts chain rotation due to its bulky cyclic structure.
*   **Effect:** This chemically raises the amorphous $T_g$ of the hard phase from ~60°C (neat PLA) to ~75–85°C, improving heat resistance for packaging.
*   **Biodegradability:** Isosorbide is sugar-derived and the resulting polyester is biodegradable.

**3. Soft Segment (Ductile Phase):**
**Poly(butylene succinate) (PBS) or Poly($\epsilon$-caprolactone) (PCL).**
*   **Composition:** Aliphatic polyester blocks.
*   **Function:** These segments have a low $T_g$ (approx. -30°C to -60°C) and high flexibility.
*   **Effect:** They form dispersed domains within the hard matrix. Under stress, these domains induce shear yielding and crazing, dissipating energy and preventing brittle fracture, thus maintaining ductility.
*   **Biodegradability:** Both PBS and PCL are well-established biodegradable polyesters.

**4. Linker/Coupling:**
**Hexamethylene diisocyanate (HDI)** or **transesterification catalysis**.
*   Used to connect the hard and soft prepolymers into a multiblock structure while maintaining biodegradability (urethane linkages in small quantities are generally acceptable in this context, or ester linkages via transesterification).

### Mechanism of Action
1.  **Thermal Resistance:** The continuous PLI hard phase creates a rigid matrix with a restricted free volume, elevating the bulk $T_g$.
2.  **Toughening:** The dispersed PBS/PCL soft phases act as impact modifiers. Because the phases are chemically linked (block copolymer), interfacial adhesion is strong, preventing delamination while allowing energy dissipation.
3.  **Biodegradability:** All components (Lactide, Isosorbide, Succinate, Caprolactone) are bio-based or biodegradable, ensuring the final material meets composting standards (e.g., ASTM D6400).
