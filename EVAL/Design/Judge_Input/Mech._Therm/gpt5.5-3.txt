A good PLA-based copolymer strategy for biodegradable packaging is to use a **high-$T_g$ rigid comonomer combined with a small flexible toughening segment**, preferably in a **block or segmented architecture** rather than a fully random copolymer.

## Proposed material

### **PLA-based segmented copolymer: PLA-co-polycarbonate or PLA-block-polycarbonate with flexible aliphatic ester segments**

One promising design is:

\[
\text{PLA}-b-\left[\text{rigid bio-based carbonate/ester segment}\right]-b-\text{PLA}
\]

with a small amount of flexible biodegradable comonomer incorporated.

A practical example:

\[
\text{poly(L-lactide)}\text{-}b\text{-poly(isosorbide carbonate-co-caprolactone)}\text{-}b\text{-poly(L-lactide)}
\]

or more generally:

\[
\text{PLLA-co-isosorbide carbonate-co-caprolactone}
\]

## Design concept

### 1. Use **PLLA** as the main biodegradable matrix

Poly(L-lactide), PLLA, provides:

- Biodegradability and compostability potential  
- Good stiffness and strength  
- Renewable sourcing  
- Existing processability in packaging films and thermoformed articles  

However, neat PLA has:

- $T_g \approx 55$–$60^\circ\text{C}$
- Brittleness
- Low heat resistance

The goal is to raise $T_g$ to perhaps **70–90°C** while preserving elongation and toughness.

---

### 2. Add a rigid, bio-based high-$T_g$ comonomer

A strong candidate is **isosorbide**, a rigid bicyclic diol derived from glucose.

Incorporating isosorbide carbonate or isosorbide ester units into PLA can increase chain rigidity and raise $T_g$.

Example rigid unit:

\[
\text{isosorbide carbonate}
\]

Benefits:

- Bio-based
- High rigidity due to fused-ring structure
- Raises $T_g$
- Maintains transparency when well incorporated
- Can remain hydrolytically or enzymatically degradable depending on composition

A target composition could be:

\[
70\text{–}85 \,\text{mol\% lactide}
\]
\[
10\text{–}25 \,\text{mol\% isosorbide carbonate/ester}
\]
\[
5\text{–}15 \,\text{mol\% flexible aliphatic segment}
\]

---

### 3. Add a small flexible biodegradable segment for ductility

To avoid making the polymer too brittle, incorporate a flexible aliphatic comonomer such as:

- $\varepsilon$-caprolactone
- trimethylene carbonate
- adipate/sebacate units
- succinate units
- poly(butylene succinate)-like soft segments

For example, **caprolactone** lowers crystallinity and improves elongation at break, while still being biodegradable.

The flexible segment should be limited, because too much will reduce $T_g$.

A good target is:

\[
5\text{–}10 \,\text{mol\% } \varepsilon\text{-caprolactone}
\]

or a short soft block with molecular weight around:

\[
M_n \approx 1{,}000\text{–}5{,}000 \ \text{g/mol}
\]

---

## Recommended copolymer architecture

### Best option: segmented/block copolymer

A useful architecture is:

\[
\text{PLLA hard block} - \text{flexible biodegradable soft block} - \text{PLLA hard block}
\]

with rigid isosorbide-containing units incorporated into the PLA-rich hard phase.

Example:

\[
\text{P(LA-co-IC)}-b-\text{PCL}-b-\text{P(LA-co-IC)}
\]

where:

- LA = lactide
- IC = isosorbide carbonate
- PCL = polycaprolactone soft segment

This gives:

- Higher $T_g$ from the PLA/isosorbide-rich phase
- Ductility from the PCL soft phase
- Physical crosslinking or microphase separation from hard PLA-rich domains
- Better toughness than a fully random brittle copolymer

---

## Suggested composition

A starting formulation could be:

| Component | Role | Suggested amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| L-lactide | Main biodegradable matrix | 70–80 mol% |
| Isosorbide carbonate/ester unit | Raises $T_g$ and stiffness | 15–20 mol% |
| $\varepsilon$-caprolactone or trimethylene carbonate | Improves ductility | 5–10 mol% |

Expected property direction:

| Property | Expected result |
|---|---|
| $T_g$ | Increased from ~60°C to ~70–85°C |
| Elongation at break | Higher than neat PLA |
| Modulus | Slightly lower or similar to PLA |
| Toughness | Improved |
| Transparency | Potentially retained if phase domains are small |
| Biodegradability | Maintained, though likely slower than PLA |

---

## Possible synthesis route

### Step 1: Prepare hydroxyl-terminated flexible oligomer

For example:

\[
\text{HO-PCL-OH}
\]

from ring-opening polymerization of $\varepsilon$-caprolactone.

### Step 2: Chain-extend with lactide and isosorbide carbonate precursor

Use ring-opening polymerization or polycondensation to form:

\[
\text{P(LA-co-IC)}-b-\text{PCL}-b-\text{P(LA-co-IC)}
\]

Catalysts could include:

- tin octoate, common but less ideal for food packaging
- zinc lactate
- magnesium-based catalysts
- organocatalysts such as TBD or DBU systems

For biodegradable packaging, lower-toxicity catalysts such as **zinc, magnesium, or organocatalysts** would be preferred.

---

## Key design tradeoff

Increasing $T_g$ usually requires rigid or bulky units, but these often reduce ductility. The solution is to separate the functions:

- **Rigid isosorbide-containing PLA-rich phase** raises $T_g$
- **Flexible biodegradable soft segment** absorbs strain and improves toughness
- **Block/segmented structure** maintains mechanical balance better than a random copolymer

---

## Final recommended design

A strong candidate would be:

\[
\boxed{
\text{P(L-lactide-co-isosorbide carbonate)}-b-\text{PCL}-b-\text{P(L-lactide-co-isosorbide carbonate)}
}
\]

with approximately:

\[
75 \,\text{mol\% L-lactide}
\]
\[
15\text{–}20 \,\text{mol\% isosorbide carbonate}
\]
\[
5\text{–}10 \,\text{mol\% caprolactone}
\]

This design should raise the glass-transition temperature compared with neat PLA while maintaining or improving ductility for biodegradable packaging applications.
