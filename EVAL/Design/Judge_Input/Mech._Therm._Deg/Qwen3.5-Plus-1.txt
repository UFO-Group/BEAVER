To simultaneously increase the glass transition temperature ($T_g$), preserve toughness, and maintain a clinically acceptable degradation rate, the optimal design is a **Segmented Multiblock Poly(ester amide) (PEA)**.

### 1. Copolymer Design Architecture
This design utilizes a thermoplastic elastomer architecture, combining a rigid, high-$T_g$ hard segment with a rubbery, energy-dissipating soft segment.

*   **Hard Segment (High $T_g$):** **Poly(L-phenylalanine ester)** or a **Poly(L-lactide-co-L-phenylalanine)** block.
    *   *Monomer Source:* **L-Phenylalanine** (an amino acid) and a diol (e.g., 1,4-butanediol).
*   **Soft Segment (Toughness):** **Poly(trimethylene carbonate) (PTMC)**.
    *   *Monomer Source:* **1,3-Dioxan-2-one**.
*   **Linkages:** The segments are linked via ester and amide bonds, forming a multiblock structure $(Hard-Soft)_n$.

### 2. Mechanism of Action

#### Increasing $T_g$
Standard biodegradable polyesters like PLGA have a $T_g$ of ~45°C, and PLLA is ~60°C. This design targets a $T_g$ of **80–100°C** through two mechanisms:
*   **Steric Hindrance:** The bulky **benzyl side group** of the phenylalanine monomer restricts the rotation of the polymer backbone.
*   **Hydrogen Bonding:** The incorporation of **amide linkages** (-NH-CO-) creates a network of inter-chain hydrogen bonds. These act as physical crosslinks that significantly increase the energy required for segmental motion.

#### Preserving Toughness
High-$T_g$ polymers are typically brittle. Toughness is preserved via:
*   **Microphase Separation:** The rigid hard segments form physical domains that provide structural integrity, while the rubbery PTMC soft segments ($T_g \approx -15^\circ\text{C}$) act as an internal impact modifier.
*   **Sacrificial Bonds:** Under stress, the hydrogen bonds in the hard segment can reversibly break and reform. This dissipates mechanical energy (hysteresis) and prevents catastrophic crack propagation.

#### Clinically Acceptable Degradation
*   **Hydrolytic Degradation:** The ester bonds in the PTMC and polyester segments undergo hydrolysis, providing a predictable bulk degradation rate tunable from months to years.
*   **Biocompatibility:** The degradation products are **L-phenylalanine** (a natural amino acid), **1,4-butanediol** (metabolized to succinate), and **1,3-propanediol**. These are naturally metabolized or excreted by the body, ensuring minimal inflammatory response.

### 3. Summary of Properties

| Property | Mechanism | Target Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$T_g$** | Amide H-bonding + Benzyl steric bulk | **High** (>80°C) |
| **Toughness** | PTMC soft domains + H-bond dissipation | **High** (Ductile) |
| **Degradation** | Ester hydrolysis + Amide enzymatic cleavage | **Tunable** (Months/Years) |
| **Biocompatibility** | Amino acid and diol metabolites | **Safe** |
