To simultaneously increase the glass transition temperature (\(T_g\)), preserve toughness, and maintain a clinically acceptable degradation profile, a multi-block thermoplastic elastomer based on **poly(DTE carbonate)** hard segments and **poly(ε-caprolactone)** (PCL) soft segments is proposed. This design exploits microphase separation between the two immiscible blocks to decouple thermal, mechanical, and degradation properties.

---

### Proposed Copolymer Architecture

**ABA triblock copolymer**  
- **Hard end‑blocks (A):** poly(desaminotyrosyl‑tyrosine ethyl ester carbonate) – poly(DTE carbonate)  
  \( T_g \approx 90\ \degree\text{C} \); hydrolytically degradable via surface erosion without acidic by‑products.  
- **Soft mid‑block (B):** poly(ε‑caprolactone)  
  \( T_g \approx -60\ \degree\text{C} \); high elongation at break (>500 %), provides elastomeric character.  
- **Composition:** 20–40 wt% hard segments, giving a co‑continuous or dispersed‑hard‑domain morphology.

---

### How the Design Meets Each Criterion

#### 1. Increased Glass Transition Temperature
- The poly(DTE carbonate) blocks have a \(T_g\) of ~90 °C, significantly **higher than that of poly(L‑lactide) (55–65 °C)** and most conventional aliphatic polyesters.
- Because the blocks are immiscible, the material exhibits **two distinct \(T_g\) values** in dynamic mechanical analysis: a low \(T_g\) from PCL (−60 °C) and a high \(T_g\) from the poly(DTE carbonate) phase (~90 °C).  
- The **upper \(T_g\) dictates the thermal stability and shape retention** of the device at physiological temperature (37 °C) and even during steam sterilisation, making it superior to single‑phase aliphatic polyesters.

#### 2. Preserved Toughness
- Microphase separation creates **hard domains (physical crosslinks)** dispersed in a continuous soft PCL matrix (or co‑continuous).  
- Under load, the rubbery PCL phase undergoes extensive **cavitation and shear yielding**, dissipating energy and preventing brittle fracture.  
- The hard domains reinforce the network without catastrophic crack propagation, resulting in **high elongation at break (>300 %) and notched impact resistance** comparable to commercial thermoplastic elastomers.  
- This overcomes the inherent brittleness of high‑\(T_g\) homopolymers like poly(DTE carbonate) alone.

#### 3. Clinically Acceptable Degradation Rate
- **Poly(DTE carbonate):** Degrades by slow hydrolysis of the carbonate linkage, releasing **non‑acidic, biocompatible degradation products** (desaminotyrosyl‑tyrosine, ethanol, CO₂). The absence of autocatalysis avoids rapid pH drops and preserves mechanical integrity longer.  
- **PCL:** Degrades by slow ester hydrolysis; its hydrophobic nature and low water uptake further retard degradation.  
- The overall mass‑loss profile can be **tuned between 6 and 24 months** by adjusting the block length ratio and hard‑segment content, matching typical clinical timelines for bone fixation, sutures, or drug‑eluting matrices.  
- All degradation products are metabolically inert or readily cleared, ensuring excellent biocompatibility.

---

### Summary of Advantages

| Property          | Poly(DTE carbonate)-b-PCL-b-poly(DTE carbonate) | Comparison (e.g., PLLA) |
|-------------------|--------------------------------------------------|--------------------------|
| \(T_g\) (upper)    | ~90 °C                                          | ~55–65 °C               |
| Toughness         | >300 % elongation, high impact strength          | <5 % elongation, brittle |
| Degradation rate  | 6–24 months (tunable), no acidic burst           | Weeks to years, often acidic |
| Biocompatibility  | Excellent (non‑toxic by‑products)                | Good (lactic acid)       |

This block copolymer system thus simultaneously **elevates \(T_g\) (via the high‑\(T_g\) polycarbonate hard block), maintains outstanding toughness (through the elastomeric PCL soft block), and provides a predictable, clinically useful degradation rate**.
