A good design is a **lactide-rich, segmented biodegradable copolymer** toughened with a small amount of flexible carbonate/caprolactone units and degradation-tuned with glycolide.

## Proposed copolymer

\[
\textbf{P[(L-lactide-co-glycolide)-b-(trimethylene carbonate-co-}\varepsilon\textbf{-caprolactone)-b-(L-lactide-co-glycolide)]}
\]

A practical starting composition would be:

- **L-lactide: 65–75 mol%**
- **glycolide: 15–25 mol%**
- **trimethylene carbonate, TMC: 5–10 mol%**
- **ε-caprolactone: 0–5 mol%**

For example:

\[
\textbf{P(LLA}_{70}\textbf{-co-GA}_{20}\textbf{)-b-P(TMC}_{7}\textbf{-co-CL}_{3}\textbf{)-b-P(LLA}_{70}\textbf{-co-GA}_{20}\textbf{)}
\]

## Why this design works

### 1. Increased \(T_g\)

The **lactide-rich hard segments** provide a relatively high glass-transition temperature.

- PLLA has \(T_g \approx 55–65^\circ\text{C}\)
- PLGA typically has \(T_g \approx 40–55^\circ\text{C}\), depending on composition
- Keeping lactide as the majority component helps maintain \(T_g\) above body temperature.

Target:

\[
T_g \approx 45–55^\circ\text{C}
\]

which is clinically useful because the polymer remains dimensionally stable at \(37^\circ\text{C}\).

### 2. Preserved toughness

Pure lactide-rich polymers can be brittle. To avoid that, introduce a small amount of flexible soft segment:

- **TMC** increases elasticity and ductility.
- **ε-caprolactone** improves toughness and elongation at break.
- A **segmented/block architecture** is better than a fully random copolymer because it allows soft domains to dissipate energy without excessively lowering the bulk \(T_g\).

The soft segment should be limited to roughly **5–15 mol% total** so that toughness improves without depressing \(T_g\) too much.

Expected mechanical effect:

- Higher elongation at break than PLLA or lactide-rich PLGA
- Improved impact resistance
- Reduced brittle fracture
- Retained tensile strength from the lactide/glycolide hard phase

### 3. Clinically acceptable degradation rate

Degradation can be tuned by the glycolide content.

- **Lactide**: slower-degrading, hydrophobic, maintains strength longer
- **Glycolide**: faster hydrolysis, increases water uptake
- **TMC/CL**: improves toughness but can slow degradation if used in excess

A composition with **15–25 mol% glycolide** should give a degradation profile in the clinically useful range for many temporary implants, roughly:

\[
\text{bulk degradation/resorption: } 3–12 \text{ months}
\]

depending on molecular weight, crystallinity, implant geometry, and processing.

## Recommended molecular/processing targets

Suggested specifications:

| Property | Target |
|---|---|
| \(T_g\) | \(45–55^\circ\text{C}\) |
| Number-average molecular weight, \(M_n\) | 50–120 kDa |
| Dispersity, Đ | 1.5–2.2 |
| Soft-segment content | 5–15 mol% |
| Glycolide content | 15–25 mol% |
| Crystallinity | low to moderate |
| Degradation time | 3–12 months |

Lower crystallinity is desirable because highly crystalline PLLA can degrade too slowly and become brittle. A mostly amorphous or weakly semicrystalline morphology is preferable for balanced toughness and degradation.

## Summary design rationale

Use:

\[
\boxed{
\text{lactide-rich PLGA hard segments + minor TMC/CL soft segments}
}
\]

This gives:

- **Higher \(T_g\)** from lactide-rich domains  
- **Toughness** from flexible carbonate/caprolactone segments  
- **Controlled clinical degradation** from glycolide-driven hydrolysis  
- **Biodegradability** through hydrolysable ester/carbonate linkages  

A strong starting formulation would be approximately:

\[
\boxed{
70\% \text{ L-lactide} / 20\% \text{ glycolide} / 7\% \text{ TMC} / 3\% \varepsilon\text{-caprolactone}
}
\]

in a **segmented triblock or multiblock architecture**.
