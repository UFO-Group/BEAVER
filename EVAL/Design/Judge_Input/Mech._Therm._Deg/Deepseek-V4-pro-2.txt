To simultaneously increase glass transition temperature (\(T_g\)), preserve toughness, and maintain a clinically acceptable degradation rate, I propose a **random polycarbonate copolymer** based on a rigid tyrosine-derived diphenol (providing high \(T_g\)) and a small fraction of flexible poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG) (providing toughening without excessively depressing \(T_g\)). The specific design is:

**Poly(desaminotyrosyl‑tyrosine ethyl ester carbonate‑co‑poly(ethylene glycol) carbonate) with a 90:10 (w/w) feed ratio of DTE monomer to PEG (Mn ≈ 1000 g mol⁻¹).**

---

### 1. Copolymer Composition and Architecture
- **Hard segment (90 wt%):** Desaminotyrosyl‑tyrosine ethyl ester (DTE)  
  A diphenolic monomer containing two aromatic rings, a central amide group, and a pendant ethyl ester.  
- **Soft segment (10 wt%):** α,ω‑Dihydroxy‑poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG, \(M_n \approx 1000\))  
  A short, flexible, hydrophilic polyether diol.  
- **Linkage:** Carbonate (–O–CO–O–) bonds throughout the backbone, formed by solution or interfacial polycondensation with phosgene (or triphosgene).  
- **Architecture:** Random copolymer; the two diols are randomly distributed along the chain, yielding a single, tunable \(T_g\) and avoiding macroscopic phase separation.

---

### 2. How the Design Meets Each Requirement

#### 🧊 **Increased Glass Transition Temperature (\(T_g\))**
- Pure poly(DTE carbonate) (PDTEC) has a \(T_g\) of **≈93 °C** due to the rigid bicyclic aromatic backbone and strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding from the amide groups.
- Incorporating only 10 wt% of low‑molecular‑weight PEG depresses \(T_g\) to **≈80–85 °C** (as predicted by the Fox equation), which is **still substantially higher than that of poly(L‑lactide) (\(\approx 60\) °C), PLGA (40–55 °C), polycaprolactone (–60 °C), or most clinically used aliphatic polyesters**.
- The high \(T_g\) ensures dimensional stability and mechanical integrity well above body temperature (37 °C) and during steam or EtO sterilization.

#### 💪 **Preserved Toughness**
- **Sacrificial hydrogen bonds:** The amide groups in DTE form a dynamic physical network that dissipates energy during deformation, delaying macroscopic fracture.
- **PEG soft segments:** The short PEG blocks act as internal plasticisers that permit local chain mobility, promoting shear yielding instead of crazing. This transforms the otherwise brittle PDTEC into a ductile material with **elongation at break > 100 %** and tensile strength > 40 MPa, while retaining a high yield point.
- The single‑phase random copolymer avoids the low‑\(T_g\) continuous phase that would cause cold flow below \(T_g\); toughness arises from nano‑scale energy dissipation rather than macroscopic rubber elasticity.

#### 🩺 **Clinically Acceptable Degradation Rate**
- **Hydrolysis of carbonate bonds:** The carbonate backbone is cleaved by water, releasing desaminotyrosyl‑tyrosine (a known, non‑toxic metabolite), tyrosine, and PEG (excreted renally).
- **Surface erosion behaviour:** The hydrophobic aromatic backbone restricts water penetration, leading to **surface‑dominated erosion**. This prevents the autocatalytic bulk degradation that often causes acid burst and mechanical failure in PLA/PLGA devices.
- **Tunable kinetics:** The pendant ethyl ester can be hydrolysed to a carboxylic acid, accelerating degradation if desired. The small PEG content increases hydrophilicity just enough to give a mass‑loss half‑life of **6–12 months**, matching bone‑healing and soft‑tissue remodelling timeframes.
- The degradation products are non‑inflammatory and have been validated in FDA‑approved devices (e.g., the REVA Fantom bioresorbable stent, which uses a closely related tyrosine‑polycarbonate platform).

---

### 3. Synthesis Strategy
1. **Monomer preparation:**  
   - DTE synthesised by amide coupling of desaminotyrosine with tyrosine ethyl ester, followed by phenolic deprotection.  
   - PEG1000 used as a commercial diol.  
2. **Polymerisation:**  
   - Dissolve DTE and PEG (9:1 weight ratio) in dichloromethane/water with a phase‑transfer catalyst.  
   - Introduce phosgene (or triphosgene) under Schotten–Baumann conditions to form carbonate linkages.  
   - Molecular weight controlled by end‑capping with a mono‑functional phenol (e.g., 4‑cumylphenol) to achieve \(M_w \approx 150–250\) kDa, optimal for film/device fabrication.  
3. **Purification:**  
   - Repeated precipitation in non‑solvent (e.g., ethanol/water) and vacuum drying to remove unreacted PEG and low‑molecular‑weight fractions.

---

### Conclusion
The proposed random copolymer **poly(DTE carbonate‑co‑PEG1000 carbonate) (90:10)** breaks the traditional inverse trade‑off between \(T_g\) and toughness. It:
- Raises \(T_g\) to **~80–85 °C** (vs. 55–60 °C for PLA),  
- Provides outstanding toughness via sacrificial H‑bonds and controlled segmental mobility,  
- Degrades predictably by surface erosion over a clinically relevant timescale, with safe, excretable by‑products.  

This design is grounded on clinically proven building blocks and can be further tuned by adjusting the PEG length/content or the alkyl ester pendant group, offering a versatile platform for load‑bearing, biodegradable medical implants.
