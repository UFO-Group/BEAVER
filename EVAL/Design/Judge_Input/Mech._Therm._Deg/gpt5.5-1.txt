A good design is a **segmented lactide-rich biodegradable copolymer** in which a high-\(T_g\) hard phase raises thermal performance, while a low-\(T_g\) ductile phase preserves toughness and a small hydrolytically labile component tunes degradation.

## Proposed copolymer

\[
\textbf{PLLA-rich hard blocks}-b-\textbf{P(TMC-co-CL-co-GA) soft blocks}-b-\textbf{PLLA-rich hard blocks}
\]

A practical target composition could be:

| Component | Approx. mol% | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| L-lactide / D,L-lactide | 60–70% | Raises \(T_g\), provides strength and stiffness |
| Trimethylene carbonate, TMC | 15–25% | Adds ductility and toughness; reduces brittleness |
| ε-caprolactone, CL | 5–15% | Improves elongation and crack resistance |
| Glycolide, GA | 3–8% | Accelerates hydrolytic degradation to clinically useful timescale |

A representative formulation:

\[
\textbf{P(LLA-co-DLLA)}_{65}
-
\textbf{P(TMC-co-CL-co-GA)}_{35}
\]

with roughly:

\[
65\% \text{ lactide} / 20\% \text{ TMC} / 10\% \text{ CL} / 5\% \text{ GA}
\]

## Design rationale

### 1. Increase \(T_g\)

Lactide-rich segments have a relatively high glass transition:

\[
T_g(\text{PLA}) \approx 55–65^\circ\text{C}
\]

Increasing the lactide fraction raises the effective \(T_g\) compared with softer biodegradable polymers such as PCL or PTMC. Using mostly L-lactide with a small amount of D,L-lactide helps control crystallinity so the material is not overly brittle.

### 2. Preserve toughness

Pure PLA has good stiffness but poor toughness. Incorporating a soft biodegradable phase such as TMC and/or caprolactone provides:

- higher elongation at break,
- improved impact resistance,
- better crack blunting,
- reduced catastrophic brittle failure.

A block or segmented architecture is preferable to a purely random copolymer because the PLA-rich domains act as physical crosslinks, while the TMC/CL-rich domains dissipate energy.

### 3. Maintain clinically acceptable degradation

PLA alone may degrade slowly and can become brittle during degradation. PCL degrades even more slowly. Adding a small amount of glycolide increases hydrolysis rate because PGA-rich ester bonds are more hydrophilic and labile.

The degradation rate can be tuned by adjusting:

- glycolide content,
- molecular weight,
- lactide stereochemistry,
- crystallinity,
- block length,
- device geometry.

For many resorbable medical applications, a useful target would be substantial mechanical retention for several weeks to months, followed by mass loss over approximately **6–18 months**, depending on the indication.

## Suggested structure

A useful architecture would be:

\[
\text{PLLA}_{n}-b-\text{P(TMC-co-CL-co-GA)}_{m}-b-\text{PLLA}_{n}
\]

where:

- the PLLA blocks provide high \(T_g\), strength, and dimensional stability,
- the middle TMC/CL/GA block provides toughness and degradation tuning,
- the triblock structure gives elastomer-like energy dissipation without sacrificing too much stiffness.

## Expected property profile

Compared with neat PLA:

| Property | Expected effect |
|---|---|
| \(T_g\) | Maintained near or modestly below PLA, higher than PCL/PTMC-based materials |
| Toughness | Significantly improved |
| Elongation at break | Increased |
| Brittleness | Reduced |
| Degradation rate | Faster than PLA/PCL, slower and less abrupt than PGA-rich polymers |
| Biocompatibility | Based on known resorbable monomers |

## Final recommendation

A strong candidate is a **lactide-rich segmented copolymer containing TMC/CL soft segments and a small glycolide fraction**, for example:

\[
\boxed{
\text{PLLA}_{65}\text{-co-}\text{TMC}_{20}\text{-co-}\text{CL}_{10}\text{-co-}\text{GA}_{5}
}
\]

preferably arranged as a **PLA-rich hard-block / TMC-CL-GA soft-block segmented copolymer**. This design raises or maintains a clinically useful \(T_g\), avoids PLA-like brittleness, and provides a tunable biodegradable profile suitable for resorbable medical devices.
