A good design target is a **semi-crystalline aliphatic–aromatic biodegradable copolyester** that raises heat resistance via limited rigid/ordered segments, while retaining enough hydrolysable aliphatic ester content and amorphous accessibility for degradation.

## Proposed polymer

### **P(BS-co-BF-co-BA) with controlled aromatic content**
A random or lightly blocky copolyester based on:

- **Succinic acid or dimethyl succinate**  
- **1,4-butanediol**
- A moderate fraction of **2,5-furandicarboxylic acid** or dimethyl 2,5-furandicarboxylate  
- A small fraction of **adipic acid** or another flexible aliphatic diacid

Conceptually:

\[
\text{poly(butylene succinate-co-butylene furanoate-co-butylene adipate)}
\]

or abbreviated:

\[
\mathrm{P(BS\text{-}co\text{-}BF\text{-}co\text{-}BA)}
\]

A useful starting composition could be approximately:

- **60–75 mol% butylene succinate units**
- **15–30 mol% butylene furanoate units**
- **5–15 mol% butylene adipate units**

relative to total diacid-derived units.

## Why this design works

### 1. Improved heat resistance
Poly(butylene succinate), PBS, is biodegradable but has only moderate thermal performance. Introducing **butylene furanoate units** increases chain rigidity because the furan ring is stiff and polar. This can:

- Increase **glass transition temperature, Tg**
- Increase modulus at elevated temperature
- Improve dimensional stability
- Potentially improve crystallization behavior if composition is optimized

Furan-based aromatic units are also attractive because they can be bio-based and generally less persistent than highly aromatic terephthalate-rich structures.

### 2. Avoiding excessive suppression of degradation
If too many rigid aromatic units are added, water uptake and chain mobility decrease, slowing hydrolysis and microbial degradation. To avoid this, the design keeps the furan content moderate and retains a high fraction of **aliphatic ester units**, especially succinate and adipate.

The **adipate comonomer** helps by:

- Increasing chain flexibility
- Reducing overly high crystallinity
- Increasing amorphous fraction accessibility
- Maintaining hydrolytic and enzymatic degradability

This balances the heat-resistance gain from furan units with degradation accessibility from flexible aliphatic segments.

## Recommended composition window

A practical first formulation would be:

\[
\mathrm{P(BS_{70}\text{-}BF_{20}\text{-}BA_{10})}
\]

where the subscripts represent approximate molar percentages of diacid-derived units.

This composition should give better heat resistance than PBS while avoiding the large degradation slowdown expected from high aromatic content.

If more heat resistance is needed, increase BF to 25–30 mol%.  
If degradation is too slow, reduce BF to 10–15 mol% or increase BA to 15–20 mol%.

## Structural features to control

### Keep the copolymer mostly random
A highly blocky structure may form long rigid crystalline domains that resist water penetration. A more random architecture helps maintain biodegradable amorphous regions.

### Avoid excessive molecular weight
Very high molecular weight improves mechanical strength but slows degradation. A moderate number-average molecular weight, for example:

\[
M_n \approx 40{,}000\text{–}80{,}000 \ \mathrm{g/mol}
\]

is a reasonable target for films, molded parts, or packaging applications.

### Avoid high crosslink density
Crosslinking improves heat resistance but strongly suppresses degradation. If crosslinking is used, it should be very light and preferably hydrolysable or reversible.

## Possible synthesis route

A standard two-step melt polycondensation can be used:

1. **Esterification or transesterification**
   - Dimethyl succinate
   - Dimethyl 2,5-furandicarboxylate
   - Dimethyl adipate
   - 1,4-butanediol excess

2. **Polycondensation**
   - Reduced pressure
   - Elevated temperature
   - Titanium butoxide, zinc acetate, or tin-based catalyst, depending on regulatory constraints

The final composition can be tuned by feed ratio and verified by NMR.

## Expected property changes

Compared with PBS:

| Property | Expected change |
|---|---|
| Tg | Increases |
| Heat deflection temperature | Increases |
| Modulus at elevated temperature | Increases |
| Crystallinity | Moderate, depending on composition |
| Hydrolytic degradation | Slightly slower than PBS |
| Enzymatic degradation | Retained if aliphatic content remains high |
| Compostability | More likely retained at moderate BF content |

## Characterization plan

To confirm the design:

- **DSC**: Tg, Tm, crystallinity, crystallization rate  
- **TGA**: thermal stability  
- **DMA**: modulus retention near use temperature  
- **WAXD/SAXS**: crystal structure and lamellar morphology  
- **GPC/SEC**: molecular weight and degradation kinetics  
- **NMR**: copolymer composition and randomness  
- **Hydrolysis tests**: buffered water at 37–60°C  
- **Compost or enzymatic degradation tests**: mass loss, molecular-weight loss, CO₂ evolution  
- **Mechanical testing**: tensile strength, elongation, toughness before and after aging

## Alternative design options

### PLA-based option
Use a stereocomplex PLA-rich material with a small amount of degradable flexible copolyester. PLA stereocomplexes have improved heat resistance, but crystallinity can strongly slow degradation, so this must be carefully controlled.

### PBS/PBF blend or copolymer
A PBS blended or copolymerized with a limited amount of poly(butylene furanoate) can improve thermal properties, but a random copolymer is preferable if degradation retention is important.

### PBS-co-PBT-type materials
Terephthalate units improve heat resistance strongly, as in PBAT-type materials, but high terephthalate content can reduce biodegradation. Furan units are generally a better choice for a more bio-based and potentially more degradable design.

## Final recommendation

Use a **random poly(butylene succinate-co-butylene furanoate-co-butylene adipate)** with approximately:

\[
\boxed{\mathrm{BS/BF/BA = 70/20/10}}
\]

This design increases heat resistance through moderate furan-ring incorporation while preserving degradation kinetics through dominant aliphatic ester content, flexible adipate units, moderate crystallinity, and a non-crosslinked hydrolysable backbone.
