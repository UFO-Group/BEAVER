A good balanced design would be a **mostly aliphatic, partially rigidified random copolyester**, rather than a highly aromatic polyester. The goal is to raise glass-transition temperature, melting point, and heat-deflection temperature while keeping enough hydrolysable/aliphatic ester content and moderate crystallinity for biodegradation.

## Proposed material

### Random copolyester:

\[
\textbf{poly(butylene succinate-co-butylene furandicarboxylate-co-butylene adipate)}
\]

abbreviated as:

\[
\textbf{PB(S-co-F-co-A)}
\]

with an approximate molar composition:

- **60–70 mol% butylene succinate units**
- **10–20 mol% butylene 2,5-furandicarboxylate units**
- **10–20 mol% butylene adipate units**

A representative starting composition would be:

\[
\textbf{PBSAF: 65/15/20}
\]

meaning 65 mol% succinate, 15 mol% furandicarboxylate, and 20 mol% adipate in the diacid component.

---

## Structural rationale

### 1. Heat resistance improvement

The **2,5-furandicarboxylate, FDCA, unit** is rigid and bio-based. Incorporating a modest amount of FDCA increases:

- glass-transition temperature, \(T_g\)
- chain stiffness
- dimensional stability at elevated temperature
- heat-deflection temperature

FDCA is useful because it provides rigidity similar to aromatic terephthalate units, but can be obtained from renewable sugars.

The rigid furan ring restricts chain mobility:

\[
\text{flexible aliphatic chain} \rightarrow \text{higher mobility}
\]

\[
\text{furan-containing chain} \rightarrow \text{lower mobility, higher } T_g
\]

So the FDCA content improves heat resistance without needing to make the polyester fully aromatic.

---

### 2. Maintaining biodegradation kinetics

To avoid excessively suppressing degradation, the polymer should remain **predominantly aliphatic**.

The **butylene succinate** and **butylene adipate** units provide:

- hydrolysable ester bonds
- flexible segments accessible to water and enzymes
- biodegradability similar to PBS/PBSA-type materials

The adipate units are especially useful because they reduce excessive crystallinity. Very high crystallinity can slow degradation by limiting water penetration and enzyme access. A controlled amount of adipate keeps the material less densely packed.

So the balance is:

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| Butylene succinate | semicrystalline biodegradable backbone, mechanical strength |
| Butylene adipate | flexibility, faster hydrolysis, reduced crystallinity |
| Butylene furandicarboxylate | rigidity, higher \(T_g\), improved heat resistance |

---

## Why not use too much rigid/aromatic content?

If FDCA or terephthalate content is too high, the polyester may become too rigid and crystalline or too hydrophobic, leading to slower degradation.

For example:

\[
>30\text{–}40 \text{ mol% FDCA}
\]

could significantly suppress biodegradation because of:

- reduced chain mobility
- increased hydrophobicity
- tighter packing
- lower enzyme accessibility

Therefore, keeping FDCA around **10–20 mol%** is a reasonable compromise.

---

## Target properties

A well-designed PB(S-co-F-co-A) composition could aim for:

| Property | Target |
|---|---|
| \(T_g\) | higher than PBS/PBSA, ideally near or above room temperature |
| \(T_m\) | 100–130°C range depending on crystallinity |
| Heat-deflection temperature | improved relative to PBSA |
| Biodegradation | slower than PBSA but still significant under composting conditions |
| Crystallinity | moderate, not excessive |
| Tensile behavior | tougher than PLA, stiffer than PBSA |

## Synthetic route

A typical synthesis would use **melt polycondensation**.

### Monomers

- 1,4-butanediol
- succinic acid or dimethyl succinate
- adipic acid or dimethyl adipate
- 2,5-furandicarboxylic acid or dimethyl 2,5-furandicarboxylate

### General process

1. **Esterification or transesterification**  
   React diacid/dimethyl ester mixture with excess 1,4-butanediol.

2. **Polycondensation under vacuum**  
   Remove excess diol at elevated temperature under reduced pressure.

3. **Catalyst**  
   Use a relatively low-toxicity catalyst such as titanium butoxide, zinc acetate, or tin-free alternatives if biomedical/packaging use is targeted.

4. **Optional chain extension**  
   A small amount of chain extender, such as multifunctional epoxide or diisocyanate, may be used to raise molecular weight, but it should be minimized because excessive branching/crosslinking can slow biodegradation.

---

## Design controls to tune degradation

To prevent degradation from becoming too slow:

### Keep FDCA content moderate

Recommended range:

\[
10\text{–}20 \text{ mol%}
\]

### Include adipate units

Adipate disrupts tight packing and increases hydrolysis rate.

Recommended range:

\[
10\text{–}25 \text{ mol%}
\]

### Avoid excessive crystallinity

A crystallinity level around:

\[
25\text{–}45\%
\]

is likely better than very high crystallinity for balanced performance.

### Keep molecular weight high enough for performance but not extreme

A useful number-average molecular weight range might be:

\[
M_n \approx 40{,}000\text{–}80{,}000 \ \text{g/mol}
\]

Very high molecular weight can improve mechanical properties but slow degradation.

---

## Recommended formulation

A strong first formulation would be:

\[
\boxed{
\text{poly(butylene succinate}_{0.65}
\text{-co-butylene furandicarboxylate}_{0.15}
\text{-co-butylene adipate}_{0.20})
}
\]

This should provide:

- improved heat resistance from the furan ring
- biodegradability from the aliphatic ester backbone
- controlled crystallinity from the adipate comonomer
- less degradation suppression than highly aromatic copolyesters such as high-terephthalate PBAT-like systems

---

## Evaluation plan

To confirm the design, test:

1. **DSC**  
   Measure \(T_g\), \(T_m\), crystallization temperature, and crystallinity.

2. **DMA**  
   Determine storage modulus retention above room temperature.

3. **HDT/Vicat softening temperature**  
   Quantify heat resistance.

4. **Hydrolytic degradation**  
   Incubate films in phosphate buffer at 37°C and 58°C.

5. **Compost degradation**  
   Evaluate mass loss and molecular-weight decrease under industrial composting conditions.

6. **GPC/SEC**  
   Monitor molecular-weight reduction during degradation.

7. **SEM**  
   Observe surface erosion and microbial attack.

---

### In short

Use a **PBS/PBSA-type biodegradable polyester lightly rigidified with FDCA**. A composition around **65% succinate / 15% furandicarboxylate / 20% adipate** should improve heat resistance while retaining enough aliphatic, hydrolysable, enzyme-accessible structure to avoid severely suppressing biodegradation.
